A Klex Bot works in external environments connected through MCP (Model Context Protocol). An MCP server can expose a full computer or an app such as GitHub or Google Workspace. Computers are connected through MCP too.

The Bot's own environment keeps it running and manages its memory. Connected computers and apps are where it carries out work.

## Choose what to connect

- **Computers** provide a connected environment where the Bot can work.
- **Apps** expose an app environment through an MCP server.

Klex offers a range of built-in MCP servers. You can also connect other MCP servers. Which environments to connect depends on the work you want the Bot to do.

## Manage a Bot's access

Connect the apps and computers your Bot needs for its job. For most MCPs, it acts through its own account or identity, with permissions configured for that Bot.

<Tabs syncKey="hosting" hash={false}>
<Tab title="Cloud-hosted">

Open your Bot in Klex Cloud and use **Connectors** to manage its app connections and **Computers** to manage its connected computers. You can also review its **Activity**.

</Tab>
<Tab title="Self-hosted">

In the Klex app, use **Settings → MCP Servers** to manage server connections. See [MCP and Klex Bots](/bot/concepts/mcp#add-a-remote-server-in-the-self-hosted-app) for the steps. Use **Settings → Logs** to review local diagnostics.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

Configure permissions for the Bot's accounts and connected apps directly in those accounts and apps. Connecting a server and granting an account access are distinct decisions. Check both when deciding what a Bot can reach and do.

For the bigger picture, see [How Klex Bots work](/bot/concepts/how-bots-work). For where chats and memory are stored, see [the data directory](/bot/concepts/data-directory).
