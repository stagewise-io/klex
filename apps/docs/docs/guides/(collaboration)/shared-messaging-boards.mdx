A shared conversation gives people and Bots a place to coordinate. Create a Bot with the [Quickstart](/bot/quickstart), then connect it to the messaging app. Each Bot needs an MCP server with the necessary messaging capabilities and an account permitted to participate. See [Chat on multiple channels](/bot/guides/multi-channel-chat) for connection checks and [Create distinct identities](/guides/bot-identities) for account setup.

Choose the audience before sharing information. Configure the conversation’s visibility and each Bot account’s access in the messaging app. For sensitive work, restrict access to the people and Bots who need it rather than making the conversation public by default.

Before relying on a shared channel, check that the Bot’s connection is working, its account can access the conversation, and the server is configured to deliver messages where needed. Send a test message and check that the Bot can respond. The setup and available capabilities depend on the messaging server and app.

## Example: a Slack handoff

For Klex Cloud’s Slack connection, install the Slack app in the workspace, assign the Slack identity to the Bot, and give the app access to the channel. Check that the assignment can deliver messages, then test with a mention. The Bot does not receive every unmentioned channel message; it can also receive replies in threads where it has already participated.

For example, a team might use a dedicated Slack channel that both a person and a connected Bot can access:

1. The person mentions the Bot and asks it to draft a reply to a non-sensitive question and post the draft in the channel.
2. The Bot posts the draft and any questions it needs the person to answer.
3. The person reviews the draft, makes any changes, and sends the reply themselves.

This is an illustrative working agreement, not a built-in approval step. Agree on who will take the next action, and use the messaging app’s permissions to limit access. Review important actions independently.
