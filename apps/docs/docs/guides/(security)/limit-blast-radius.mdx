Limit the potential impact of a compromised Bot by limiting the access available to its accounts and connected environments. The right boundaries depend on the apps, computers, and data involved; a Bot’s instructions alone are not an access control.

## Reduce exposure

- Use a distinct account for the Bot where the connected app supports it. Give it only the resources and permissions needed for its work.
- Separate sensitive resources from general-purpose work. Avoid connecting production systems or sensitive data unless the Bot needs them.
- Restrict who can reach the Bot and the conversations or tools it uses through the access controls available in those services.
- Review permissions to share information, modify settings, and perform destructive actions. Use a separate approval process for high-impact changes.
- Review access periodically and remove connections and grants that are no longer needed.

See [Use Klex Bots securely](/guides/secure-bot-usage) for an account-permission checklist. These measures reduce exposure; they do not guarantee that an incident cannot occur.

## Prepare to respond

Know which accounts, connections, and environments the Bot uses before an incident. If compromise is suspected, follow your incident response process: consider stopping the Bot, revoking access in connected services, rotating affected credentials, and reviewing relevant activity. Stopping a Bot alone is not a substitute for revoking access or investigating any actions already taken.
