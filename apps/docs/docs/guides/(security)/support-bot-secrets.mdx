A customer-facing Bot should have access only to the information and actions needed to answer support requests. Do not assume that telling a Bot to keep a secret is enough to prevent disclosure.

## Reduce what the Bot can access

- Avoid giving the Bot access to production credentials, sensitive customer records, or internal documents it does not need.
- Use separate, narrowly scoped accounts for support work where the connected service permits it. Review their access to read, share, and change data.
- Keep credentials out of support conversations and examples. Use your organization’s approved credential-management process for integrations.
- Check who can contact the Bot and who can see its responses in each connected service.

## Review the workflow

Test with non-sensitive data, including requests that try to obtain information outside the intended scope. Review the results and the permissions in connected services; do not assume that a Bot will always reject a malicious request.

If a credential or sensitive information may have been exposed, follow your incident response process. Revoke or rotate affected credentials and review relevant access and activity. See [Prevent information theft](/guides/prevent-information-theft) and [Limit blast radius](/guides/limit-blast-radius).
